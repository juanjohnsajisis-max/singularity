Structure-based lipid scorer (LiON-style)

Paste ionizable-lipid SMILES, one per line (optionally name, SMILES). Each is scored inside the formulation currently set on the left, then ranked. Runs in your browser.
How reliable? Ridge regression on the formulation plus 1,024 hashed structure counts (radius-2 atom environments), trained on 18,435 LNPDB rows from 42 publications. Tested with whole publications held out: lipids tested together in one study are ranked with mean Spearman rho 0.18 (80% of 49 test experiments above zero), versus 0.08 for formulation-only. In vivo liver: rho 0.15 over 7 studies. Spleen and lung had too few studies to test. The score is a general delivery-potency percentile, not organ-specific (the target only shifts the baseline), the mass ratio is fixed at 10, and eye has no data. Use it to prioritise candidates, not to predict results.

In-silico lipid library (Michael-addition chemistry)

Builds every combination of 238 amine heads from LNPDB with 14 acrylate/acrylamide tails (every N–H reacts, as in the standard one-pot synthesis), scores all 3,332 structures in the current formulation, and ranks them. This is the LiON idea at small scale.
Read this before trusting it. The reaction rule reproduced the real LNPDB structure for 89% of known head and tail pairs (the rest involve extra modifications). The ranking comes from the same weak model as the scorer (rho 0.18 within studies), and it tends to favour molecules with many long amide tails, which are extrapolations. Synthesis, purification and toxicity are not considered. Treat the list as ideas to test, not predictions.
